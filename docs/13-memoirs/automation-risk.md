# 13.5 Automation Risk — Stale Patterns and Misfires

An automation built on a profile that has since changed will **execute confidently against the wrong reality.** The world does not pause to let the player update their library.

> A merchant class that was paranoid and easily scared fifty years ago may have hardened after a generation of conflict and political stability. The automation built on the old profile selects fear-based framing, dispatches the approach, and the target laughs it off or reports it to their guild. The player reads the failure report and has to figure out what changed.

## Connection to two-reality system

This connects directly to the [two-reality system](../07-interface/two-realities.md).

- Automations run on the **intelligence picture.**
- If the picture is wrong, the automation is wrong.
- The player's growing library of patterns is only as reliable as the intelligence that keeps it current.
- Neglecting a region's intelligence maintenance does not just mean missing new events — it means the library quietly drifts out of sync with reality.

## Rules

- The game flags automations as **'unverified'** if the underlying profile has not been refreshed within a configurable time window.
- Running an unverified automation is always the player's choice — it may still work, but the risk is theirs.
- A pattern of automation misfires in a region is a **diagnostic signal**: something has changed that the player's picture has not captured.
- **Rival societies can deliberately corrupt a player's library** by feeding false intelligence that builds an incorrect profile — a sophisticated long-game attack.
