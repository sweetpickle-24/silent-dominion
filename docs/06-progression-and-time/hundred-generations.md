# 6.5 One Hundred Generations

The full game arc from 500 BCE to the 1900s spans approximately **100 generations.** This is the game's most important design constraint and its greatest asset.

## Why it matters

- Weight shifts have real time to compound.
- Ideological infrastructure planted early pays off centuries later.
- The player's memory of their own history becomes a genuine strategic resource as the world forgets what they remember.

## Texture of play

The game is designed to keep the player meaningfully engaged on an **ongoing basis** — not a game where significant action happens once every few years. The texture of play is roughly **monthly**:

- Approving an instruction to a spy.
- Responding to a letter.
- Nudging a host.
- Deciding whether to follow a lead.

These are not all strategic pivots — most are small, tactical, and quick. The big moves (backing an empire, triggering a succession crisis, pivoting to a new power base) remain rare and weighty. But the fabric between them is constant. Over 100 generations this produces an enormous depth of play.

## What happens across an arc

- Each era spans roughly **20–25 generations** — enough time to build a position, see it tested, and see its consequences.
- A weight shift introduced in generation 5 is almost unrecognisable by generation 30 if conditions persist.
- Ideological infrastructure planted in the ancient world shapes the medieval world in ways no single actor intended.
- The player is the only entity with memory long enough to draw the full causal line from their early actions to late consequences.
