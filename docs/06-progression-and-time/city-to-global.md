# 6.4 Starting Small — The City Origin

The game does not begin with the player commanding a continental network. It begins in **a single city, with a single host, with almost no map revealed.** This is deliberate — it grounds the player in the human scale of the ancient world and teaches every mechanic at manageable depth before the world opens up.

The growth path from city to global operation is the game's long arc. Each stage takes generations and requires different tools, different types of hosts, and a fundamentally different strategic orientation.

| **Stage** | **Scale** | **Primary tools** | **Time to reach** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **City** | One city, a handful of named individuals | Direct personal relationships, one or two hosts | Game start |
| **Regional** | A city-state or small kingdom | Host network, rumour seeding, economic threads | 1–3 generations |
| **Kingdom** | A major political entity and its neighbours | Institutional footholds, ideological infrastructure | 5–10 generations |
| **Multi-kingdom** | A civilisational sphere | Proxy networks of proxies, long-horizon ideological plays | 15–25 generations |
| **Continental** | A full continent or trade system | Shadow society coordination, cycle engine manipulation | 30–50 generations |
| **Global** | The full world map | All systems simultaneously, managing weight shifts at scale | 50–100 generations |

## Design intent

- The early game teaches mechanics **by forcing them.** Not through tutorial text, but through situations that only make sense once you understand the relevant system.
- See [§28 Onboarding](../28-onboarding.md) for the full layered unlock schedule.
- Scale changes are irreversible in their upside: once a player operates continentally they have the infrastructure to go global. But they are reversible in their downside: the organisation can collapse and force the player back to regional scale.
