# Reputation and the Survival Mandate

## The reputation problem

Visibility accumulates across time, not just within a single era. If the player acts too openly in one generation, the legend of a figure who does not age enters the cultural memory of that region. Returning to the same area decades later risks recognition — not by people who knew you, but by people who heard stories about you.

- **Local legend meter** — tracks how embedded a 'mysterious figure' has become in a region's oral history.
- **Chronicle risk** — if your actions have been written down, scholars in future eras may recognise your pattern.
- **Witchcraft threshold** — exceed visible presence during certain eras and inquisitions or witch trials become active threats.
- The only remedy is **time and distance** — legends fade if you stay away long enough.

## The survival mandate

Exposure is the primary threat mechanic. The more directly the player acts, the more they register in the world's awareness — and once known, they become a target for every faction, every paranoid ruler, every rival society, and eventually the general population.

## Core tension

- You need to be **close to power** to act.
- Being close to power makes you **visible.**
- Being visible makes you a **target.**
- Being a target forces you to **go dark** — which means losing influence.

**The entire game is managing this contradiction across centuries.**
