# Cover Identities

The player wears identities the way other characters wear clothes. Every operation, every relationship, every interaction with the world happens through one of a small portfolio of established cover identities. The merchant Tertius. The scholar Eumenes. The pilgrim Marcus. Each is a complete fiction with a backstory, an apparent age, an apparent profession, and a network of relationships and locations.

**Identities are how an immortal navigates a world that does not believe in immortals.** Without them the player could not show up at a banker's office, could not travel between cities, could not meet a host face-to-face.

## Identity attributes

| **Attribute** | **What it specifies** | **Why it matters** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Name and backstory** | Who this person claims to be and how they got here | Establishes plausibility. A backstory that does not survive scrutiny burns the identity. |
| **Apparent age** | How old this identity appears to be at introduction | Determines the identity's natural lifespan — a 30-year-old can be used for ~30 years before the lack of aging becomes obvious |
| **Apparent profession** | Merchant, scholar, pilgrim, physician, etc. | Determines what social contexts the identity can naturally enter and what kinds of operations it can support |
| **Geographic association** | Cities and regions where this identity has established presence | Identities perform best where they are known. An identity used outside its home territory raises questions. |
| **Relationship anchors** | Other characters who can vouch for the identity | An identity with no one to verify their backstory is fragile. The deeper the anchor network, the more durable the identity. |
| **Accumulated legend** | What people in the relevant region remember about this identity | Long-running identities accumulate their own minor legends — useful, until the legend becomes a target. |

## Identity lifecycle

Identities have a natural lifespan and several ways to die. Managing the identity portfolio is a continuous background task.

| **End-of-identity cause** | **What triggers it** | **Consequence** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Aging out** | The identity has been in use long enough that its lack of aging is becoming noticeable | Plan a graceful exit — the identity 'retires to the country', or moves abroad, or simply fades from active use. No exposure spike. |
| **Public exposure** | The identity is publicly seen doing something memorable that draws attention | The identity is now risky to use. The legend it carries follows it into any future appearance. Often must be retired sooner than planned. |
| **Enemy identification** | A rival society or hostile actor has connected the identity to suspicious activity | The identity is burned. Cannot be used again without serious risk. May need to be killed off publicly to neutralise the threat. |
| **Backstory failure** | Someone investigates the backstory deeply enough to find the gaps | Depends on who investigated. A casual investigator can be diverted. A determined one means the identity is finished. |
| **Relationship collapse** | A key anchor relationship dies, defects, or is exposed | The identity's plausibility weakens. May still be usable in regions where the anchor was not relevant. |

## Identity management — practical guidelines

- The player typically maintains **three to five active identities** at any time, spread across different geographic and social contexts.
- Each is used for the operations its profile fits.
- The portfolio is curated continuously — retiring identities as they age, establishing new ones in advance of need, and adapting to losses when an identity burns unexpectedly.
- **Establishing a new identity takes years** of background work — a paper trail, witnesses to a backstory, relationships built up to make the identity feel established. The lieutenant layer handles most of this preparation.
- A burned identity must sometimes be killed off publicly — a staged death, a disappearance — to neutralise it as a thread investigators could pull.
- Identities can be reused in different geographic regions if their original use was localised.
- The player's legend layer (see [§10](../10-shadow-figure/README.md)) accumulates *across* identities — it tracks the shadow figure as a concept, not any specific identity.
- Sophisticated rivals may identify the player not by any single identity but by the **operational pattern** across multiple identities used in the same region — the way someone walks, drinks wine, argues. This is rare but devastating.
