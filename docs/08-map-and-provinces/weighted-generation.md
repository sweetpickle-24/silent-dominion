# 8.11 Weighted Character Generation

When history diverges from its documented path — through the player's interference or cascading world events — **the people born into that altered world are different** from the people who would have been born into the original one. The game models this through a weight system that shapes every procedurally generated character.

Weights are **regional, cultural, and generational.** They are set by the conditions of the world at the time of a character's birth and early life.

> A person born into a militarised, unstable region after a century of war has different weighted traits than one born into a prosperous mercantile republic. The player shapes conditions; conditions shape people; people shape what the player has to work with next.

## What weights affect

| **Dimension** | **Effect** |
| --- | --- |
| **Personality traits** | Ambition, paranoia, loyalty, piety, intellect, ruthlessness, curiosity — each has a probability range set by regional conditions |
| **Susceptibility** | How open a character is to specific influence types. A pious population produces characters resistant to rational argument but highly susceptible to religious framing |
| **Resistance threshold** | How hard the player has to push before a character acts. Populations that have been manipulated before produce suspicious, harder-to-move individuals |
| **Lifespan and health** | Era, regional wealth, and stability affect how long hosts remain viable |
| **Social mobility** | Whether talented low-born individuals can rise to positions where the player can use them |

## How player actions shift weights

| **Player action** | **Condition created** | **Weight shift in next generation** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Prolonged destabilisation of a region** | Chronic warfare, insecurity, institutional collapse | Ambition +, Loyalty -, Paranoia +, Piety + (seeking comfort), Intellect - (survival prioritised over learning) |
| **Elevating merchant class over nobility** | Wealth-driven social mobility, weak military culture | Mercantile traits +, Military ambition -, Independence +, Susceptibility to economic arguments + |
| **Aggressively seeding a religion for 3+ generations** | Deep cultural faith, institutional church power | Piety +, Susceptibility to religious framing +, Rationalist resistance +, Reformer potential + (faith breeds its own critics) |
| **Investing in philosophy and literacy** | Educated population, idea spread velocity high | Intellect +, Curiosity +, Resistance to crude manipulation +, Susceptibility to sophisticated ideological framing + |
| **Engineering repeated betrayals at the top** | Distrust of leadership, cynical political culture | Loyalty - across all classes, Paranoia +, Susceptibility to all influence types - (everyone expects manipulation) |
| **Sustained peace and prosperity** | Low threat environment, institutional stability | Ambition -, Complacency +, Susceptibility to disruption high (unprepared for crisis), Reform resistance + |

## Unintended consequences

The weight system produces outcomes **the player did not design.** This is intentional.

> A player who spent a century elevating merchant power to make kingdoms easier to manipulate through economic leverage may find that the next generation's merchant class has bred a culture of high independence and low deference — the exact traits that make them resistant to the shadow manipulation that created them.

## Rules

- Weights **compound across generations** — a shift in one generation amplifies in the next if conditions persist.
- **Historical figures are immune** to weight shifts — their core traits are fixed regardless of world conditions.
- The player can attempt to reverse a weight shift by changing conditions, but **a generation already born cannot be reweighted** — only managed.
- Rival societies exploit weight shifts the player creates — if you bred a paranoid generation, expect rivals to be using that paranoia against you.
