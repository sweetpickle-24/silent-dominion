# 8.7 Infrastructure — Building the World

Infrastructure is built by **every actor** in the world with the resources and motivation to do so:

- Kings build castles and administrative buildings.
- Merchants build warehouses and banks.
- The church builds cathedrals.
- Guilds build markets.
- Rival secret societies influence construction through their own networks exactly as the player can.

The map over time becomes a **visible record of every faction's long-term investment.**

## The player never builds directly

The player never builds anything directly. They create conditions that make building happen — or remove conditions that prevent it. **Three inputs must align:**

1. The perceived need for the building.
2. The resources to construct it.
3. A decision-maker who champions it.

The player's tools are aimed at all three.

## Building influence matrix

| **Building type** | **Built by** | **How the player influences it** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Castle / fortress** | Rulers, military commanders | Amplify external threat perception to create need; fund through financial network; cultivate the military advisor who proposes it |
| **Bank / counting house** | Merchant families, financial guilds | Elevate the merchant family's status; connect them to trade routes; ensure political stability that makes long-term investment viable |
| **Cathedral / major temple** | Religious institutions, pious rulers | Seed the religious movement that demands it; provide financial backing through a pious patron; cultivate the bishop who lobbies for it |
| **University / academy** | Rulers, scholars, wealthy patrons | Cultivate an intellectual patron; import scholars who create demand; tie the institution to a ruler's prestige agenda |
| **Market / trade infrastructure** | Merchant guilds, city governments | Route trade through the city to demonstrate demand; elevate guild power relative to nobility; provide founding capital |
| **Mine / quarry** | Kingdom administration, private investors | Identify the latent resource through survey intelligence; finance the initial extraction; ensure political stability for the multi-year investment |
| **Farm / irrigation** | Local landowners, kingdom administration | Seed agricultural knowledge through scholar hosts; provide capital to landowners; manipulate the tax structure to reward cultivation |

## Construction and persistence

- Infrastructure has a **construction timeline** visible on the map as a progress state — the player can see a castle being built and estimate when it will be complete.
- Rival societies can sabotage construction through the same mechanisms the player uses to enable it: bribing the supply master, disrupting the labour source, engineering the political crisis that diverts the ruler's attention and budget.
- **Completed infrastructure is permanent** unless physically destroyed in war or deliberately demolished — a bank built in 400 CE is still there in 800 CE, still providing financial access to whoever controls the city.
