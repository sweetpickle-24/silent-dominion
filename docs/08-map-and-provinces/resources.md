# 8.6 Resources and Extraction (overview)

Provinces contain resources determined by their terrain, climate, and geology. Resources are **not abstract** — they are specific extractable goods that drive the economies of kingdoms, the construction of infrastructure, and the capacity of armies.

## Latent vs. developed

Resources exist in two states:

- **Latent** — present in the province but not yet being extracted.
- **Developed** — actively extracted through built infrastructure.

Developing a latent resource requires the right building to be constructed in the province, which requires political will, labour, and capital. The player can influence all three.

## Resources overview

| **Resource** | **Terrain source** | **Required infrastructure** | **Economic and strategic effect** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Grain / crops** | Fertile plains, river deltas | Farms, irrigation channels, granaries | Population growth, army supply, famine prevention; most critical resource |
| **Timber** | Forest provinces | Logging camps, sawmills | Construction of ships, buildings, siege equipment; enables naval power |
| **Iron / metal ore** | Mountain and hill provinces | Mines, smelters, forges | Military equipment quality; arms production; construction capacity |
| **Stone / marble** | Mountain, hill, quarry sites | Quarries, transport infrastructure | Fortification and monumental construction; prestige buildings |
| **Precious metals** | Specific mountain provinces | Deep mines, refining facilities | Monetary supply; direct financial network capacity; bribing power |
| **Livestock / horses** | Plains, steppe provinces | Pastures, stables, breeding programmes | Cavalry military output; food supply; trade commodity |
| **Luxury goods** | Specific climate and trade zones | Plantations, workshops, trade infrastructure | High-value trade; court influence; financial network premium |
| **Spices / exotic goods** | Tropical and far-trade provinces | Trade posts, caravan infrastructure | Drives long-distance trade; enormous financial value in later eras |

## Strategic role

Resource control is a primary driver of political power. A kingdom that controls the only iron mines in a region has leverage over every neighbour that needs to arm soldiers. A city that sits at the junction of two major trade routes accumulates wealth that eventually produces the social complexity the player needs to operate. The player can manipulate resource flows — disrupting a kingdom's access to grain through economic operations, engineering control of a key mine through political manipulation, or accelerating the development of a latent resource to strengthen an ally's position.
