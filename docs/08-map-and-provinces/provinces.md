# 8.2 Provinces — The Atomic Map Unit

The world is divided into **provinces.** A province is a polygon of territory that is the fundamental unit of:

- Political ownership.
- Taxation.
- Military movement.
- Resource production.

Kingdoms own provinces, not individual cities. Armies move between provinces. Unrest, famine, and instability are tracked at province level. The player's intelligence coverage is assessed per province.

## Empty provinces matter

**Not every province contains a settlement.** A mountain range is a province. A desert is a province. A dense forest is a province. These empty provinces are not meaningless — they define:

- Movement corridors.
- Resource availability.
- Military chokepoints.
- Climate effects on adjacent settled provinces.

A kingdom that controls the mountain passes between two regions controls the trade and military access between them regardless of whether anyone lives in those mountains.

## Province types

| **Province type** | **Contents** | **Strategic value** | **Effect on neighbours** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Plains** | Settlements, farms, roads | High population, high agricultural yield, easy military movement | Spreads stability and trade to adjacent provinces |
| **Forest** | Timber resources, possible small settlement | Timber production, guerrilla terrain, slow movement | Reduces military mobility through the region; provides construction resources |
| **Mountains** | Mining resources, passes, fortress sites | Strategic chokepoints, metal and stone extraction, highly defensible | Controls access between regions; generates revenue if passes are taxed |
| **Desert** | Oasis trade stops, caravan routes | Low population, trade corridor value only, extreme military attrition | Forces trade routes through specific oasis nodes; limits military campaigns |
| **River delta / coast** | Ports, fishing, fertile soil | Very high trade value, highest agricultural yield, naval access | Major trade hub potential; flood risk creates periodic agricultural disruption |
| **Hills** | Mixed terrain, quarries, vineyards | Moderate agriculture, stone resources, defensible but not impassable | Buffer zones between plains and mountains; moderate movement penalty |
| **Tundra / steppe** | Nomadic population, horse breeding | Low fixed agriculture, high cavalry military output, sparse settlement | Periodic nomadic pressure on adjacent settled regions |
