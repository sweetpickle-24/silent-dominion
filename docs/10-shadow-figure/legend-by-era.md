# 10.2 How the Legend is Named

Each era and culture produces its own name and interpretation for the shadow figure. The player **does not choose these** — they emerge from the world's cultural logic applied to the evidence of the player's actions.

- A player who operates primarily through religious channels will be interpreted as a divine messenger or a devil depending on the era.
- A player who works through merchants will become the embodiment of an invisible commercial conspiracy.

## Era interpretations

| **Era** | **Common interpretation** | **Implication** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ancient world** | A daimon, genius, or divine intermediary | Treated with superstitious respect — dangerous but potentially protective. Some rulers will actively seek contact. |
| **Medieval** | A demon, fallen angel, or servant of the adversary | Active persecution if identified. The Church treats the shadow figure as an existential threat to divine order. |
| **Renaissance** | An occultist, alchemist, or leader of a secret philosophical order | Intellectual fascination alongside fear. Some scholars will try to study and document the phenomenon. |
| **Early modern** | The head of a global conspiracy; a hidden sovereign | Political paranoia. Rulers attribute their rivals' successes to shadow figure coordination. Pamphlets circulate. |
| **Modern** | The subject of serious secret society scholarship and conspiracy literature | Intellectuals debate whether the figure is real. Some will come uncomfortably close to the truth in print. |
