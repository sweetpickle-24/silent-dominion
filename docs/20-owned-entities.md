# 20. Owned Entities — Structural Power

Influence through people is **fragile.** A host dies and the relationship dies with them. A coordinator is burned and the regional network collapses. The player rebuilds from the relationship layer every generation.

**Owned entities are different.** A trading company, a banking house, a scholarly academy, a monastery — these are institutions with legal existence, physical assets, ongoing revenues, and continuity that **survives the death of any individual.** They are the player's durable infrastructure.

The player never appears in any entity's records. What they hold is **beneficial control through a structure of proxies** — a company nominally owned by merchant families, one of which is loyal to a coordinator, whose superior is a lieutenant, who takes direction from the player.

The legal owners are the proxies. The actual direction comes from above them, invisibly.

## 20.1 Entity types

| **Entity type** | **Primary output** | **Secondary output** | **Failure mode** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Trading company** | Revenue and commercial intelligence from trade route activity | Geographic coverage across port cities; operative placement under commercial cover | Internal corruption; trade route collapse; rival acquisition of key contracts |
| **Banking house** | Financial network capacity; debt leverage over rulers and institutions | Intelligence on who owes whom; financial routing for operations | Competitor banks; ruler default; investigation of backers |
| **Scholarly academy** | Ideological infrastructure; host pipeline of educated graduates | Idea propagation through graduate networks; prestige that attracts further scholars | Institutional capture by a rival ideology; loss of patron funding |
| **Monastery / religious house** | Permanent geographic intelligence node; correspondence network | Cultural influence; hospitality network for operative movement; archive access | Religious authority investigation; war damage; reformation-era dissolution |
| **Guild or craft organisation** | Urban intelligence and operative placement; economic pressure tool | Labour control; infrastructure construction influence; city politics access | Rival guild competition; city government suppression; internal factional split |
| **Noble estate or landholding** | Agricultural revenue; provincial political access; military levy capacity | Local population intelligence; road and infrastructure control in the province | War seizure; succession failure of the proxy family; royal confiscation |

## 20.2 Founding and acquiring entities

Entities can be **founded from scratch** or **acquired** through control of an existing institution.

- Founding is slower but produces an entity shaped to the player's needs.
- Acquisition is faster but the entity has existing relationships, existing culture, and existing problems that the player inherits.

### How it works

- **Founding a trading company** — establish initial capital through the financial network, identify proxy founders through the coordinator layer, secure trading licenses through a political host in the target city, build the first route.
- **Founding a banking house** — requires significant existing financial network depth, a high-trust proxy family, and a city with sufficient commercial activity to sustain lending operations.
- **Founding an academy** — requires a scholarly host to anchor the institution, a patron with political cover, and a city with existing intellectual activity — academies do not succeed in intellectual voids.
- **Acquiring an existing institution** — identify the nominal owners or governing board, cultivate them or their successors over years, gradually shift the institution's direction without triggering resistance from existing members.

## 20.3 Management model

Entities are managed through the organisation layer, **not directly by the player.** The player sets strategic direction:

- Which ports the trading company should expand to.
- Whether the banking house should extend loans to a specific kingdom.
- What curriculum the academy should emphasise.

The coordinator layer handles operational execution. The player engages with entity management at the level of consequential decisions — roughly monthly, consistent with the game's overall decision cadence.

### Event streams

Entities generate their own event streams that surface to the player through the organisation's reporting structure:

- A trading company reports a new competitor entering their primary route.
- A banking house reports that a kingdom is three payments overdue on a major loan.
- An academy reports that a brilliant student has graduated and entered court service in a strategically important city.

These are not crises — they are opportunities and decisions that require player attention at the appropriate moment.

## 20.4 Entity longevity and historical persistence

An entity founded in 400 CE **does not disappear when the political structure around it changes.**

> The Roman trading company survives the fall of Rome as a Byzantine trading company, then as an Arab trading company, then as something else entirely — its routes, its relationships, and its accumulated commercial intelligence persisting through the transformation.

The player's beneficial control may need to be re-established through new proxy arrangements after each major political upheaval, but the institution itself is a **going concern that outlasts its political context.**

This is the deepest expression of the game's core theme. Empires fall. Religions transform. Rulers die. The machine the player builds — its organisations, its entities, its infrastructure — persists.

> A banking house founded in Carthage in 300 BCE is in some meaningful sense still operating in Tunis in 1200 CE. The player is the only one who remembers the full chain.

### Rules

- Entity continuity is tracked across political transitions — the game records which current institutions descend from player-founded entities.
- Long-established entities have accumulated **institutional memory** that provides intelligence advantages — a monastery with six hundred years of correspondence archives is a uniquely powerful intelligence resource.
- Rival societies can attempt to seize, dissolve, or redirect player-owned entities — protecting key institutions from hostile takeover is an ongoing strategic responsibility.
- An entity that has existed long enough develops its **own institutional culture** that may diverge from the player's intentions — requiring periodic re-alignment through new proxy appointments.

## 20.5 Corruption within entities

Entities are subject to the **same internal corruption dynamics** as the organisation's human network.

- A trading company with a corrupt proxy director is leaking revenue and commercial intelligence simultaneously.
- A banking house whose nominal head has been approached by a rival society is extending loans in directions that serve the rival's interests.

The detection and response mechanics are identical to the [organisational corruption system](./19-internal-corruption.md).

### Special considerations

- Entities are **harder to audit than individuals** because their financial complexity provides more places to hide diversion.
- A corrupt entity can persist undetected for longer than a corrupt individual — years rather than months — because its output looks like normal commercial variance.
- **Rival-induced corruption of a key entity** is a high-priority strategic attack — it simultaneously degrades financial capacity, intelligence coverage, and operational capability in one move.
- The player can build oversight into entity governance the same way as organisational oversight: cross-checking accounts, rotating proxy directors, limiting individual financial authority.
