# Geographic and Historical Concentration

Societies are **not uniformly present everywhere.** Each has historical roots in specific regions and has expanded or contracted over the centuries. A society with deep roots in a region operates there at higher effectiveness than one that recently extended its reach.

## Origins and strongholds

- **The Architects** emerged in the late Bronze Age Near East and have always been strongest in regions with bureaucratic civilisational tradition: Egypt, Persia, China, Byzantine territory.
- **The Pyre** arose during a major collapse event — their immortal founder lived through one of history's great civilisational extinctions and built the society around the lesson they took from it. Strongest in regions experiencing or recovering from collapse.
- **The Weavers** emerged in the early Iron Age and have always concentrated their efforts on regions with strong dynastic political structures — less effective in republics and city-states.
- **The Veil** emerged from the Hellenistic intellectual tradition and is strongest wherever scholarly institutions exist; weakest in oral or anti-intellectual cultures.
- **The Compact** is the youngest — founded during a period when the Architects were close to total dominance — and is most effective in regions with multipolar political environments.

## Mechanical implication

- A society operating in its historical stronghold has higher resource generation, better host availability, and more resilient networks.
- Displacing a society from its stronghold is a multi-generation strategic goal — not a short-term operation.
- The player's own geographic centre of gravity (see [§22 Base of Operations](../22-base-and-travel/README.md)) can usefully align with or avoid specific society strongholds.
