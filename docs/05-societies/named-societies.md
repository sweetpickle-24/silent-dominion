# The Named Societies

These are the major societies operating in the game world. Their immortal status is hidden information — the player learns it through investigation. The descriptions below assume the **active-immortal** state at the typical game start.

## Philosophies and methods

| **Society** | **Core philosophy** | **Primary method** | **Natural ally when...** | **Natural enemy when...** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Architects** | Total civilisational order; a perfected hierarchy | Back the strongest centralising power; suppress dissent | Player wants stability | Player needs chaos to manoeuvre |
| **The Pyre** | Collapse and rebirth; only destruction purifies | Accelerate instability; fuel every fracture | Player wants to bring down an empire | Player is building something |
| **The Weavers** | Preserve specific bloodlines as vessels of legitimate power | Dynastic manipulation; marriage and succession | Player backs the same bloodline | Player needs to break a dynasty |
| **The Veil** | Protect and accumulate knowledge above all else | Embed scholars; protect libraries; resist book-burning | Player needs information or ideological cover | Player needs ignorance to spread |
| **The Compact** | Controlled, sustainable power — no single actor should dominate | Balance of power mechanics; fund the underdog always | Player is the underdog | Player is trying to consolidate dominance |

## Mechanically distinct gameplay

Each society does not just want different things — it **operates differently.** The Pyre and the Architects should feel completely different to fight against because their methods, their rhythms, and their organisational shapes diverge fundamentally.

| **Society** | **Network shape** | **Operational rhythm** | **Distinctive capability** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Architects** | Highly hierarchical, centralised, formal lieutenant chain | Slow, methodical, long-horizon | Extremely deep institutional infiltration; can operate inside formal structures most others cannot reach |
| **Pyre** | Cellular, distributed, deliberately fragmented for resilience | Reactive, opportunistic, fast | Can operate in collapsed regions where no one else can; thrives on chaos that paralyses other societies |
| **Weavers** | Family-shaped — organised around the bloodlines they protect | Generational, patient, deeply personal | Unique access to noble households and dynastic intelligence; can predict succession outcomes other societies cannot |
| **Veil** | Distributed across libraries, academies, and monasteries; geographically scattered | Steady, measured, focused on continuity rather than crisis | Best counter-intelligence in the game; their archive infrastructure means they remember things other societies forget |
| **Compact** | Diffuse, broad, deliberately mediocre presence in many places rather than dominant in few | Constantly active in small ways across wide geography | Unique ability to detect and disrupt consolidation; will always be working against whoever is currently dominant, including the player when the player is winning |
