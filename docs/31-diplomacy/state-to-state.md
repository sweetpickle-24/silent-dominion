# 31.1 State-to-State Relationships

Every pair of kingdoms has a **relationship score built from stacking modifiers**, following the same logic as the Paradox grand strategy model.

The score is not a fixed number — it is the net result of dozens of positive and negative factors that change continuously as events unfold. **The player never directly sets this score. They manipulate the modifiers.**

## Modifier categories

| **Modifier category** | **Examples** | **How the player affects it** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Historical grievances** | Territorial loss, broken treaties, humiliating defeats, unsettled insults | Amplify existing grievances through targeted rumours; engineer new incidents that create grievances; suppress grievances by arranging compensatory events |
| **Dynastic ties** | Marriage alliances, shared bloodlines, succession claims, legitimate kinship | Promote or block specific marriage negotiations through court hosts; engineer or prevent succession claims |
| **Trade relations** | Shared trade routes, preferred partner status, tariff disputes, embargo effects | Route or disrupt trade between kingdoms through merchant network; manufacture trade disputes through financial manipulation |
| **Religious alignment** | Shared faith, doctrinal disputes, crusade or jihad obligations, heresy accusations | Seed theological disputes through scholarly hosts; align or misalign rulers with their church through piety manipulation |
| **Recent events** | Wars, alliances, joint campaigns, diplomatic insults, territorial exchanges | Engineer incidents through operatives; suppress incidents by removing the actors before they escalate |
| **Power balance** | Relative military strength, territorial size, economic output | Indirectly through infrastructure investment, military degradation, and economic manipulation of either party |

## Threshold events

When the relationship score crosses thresholds, diplomatic states change.

- A score that drops below the **alliance threshold** triggers alliance dissolution.
- A score that crosses the **casus belli threshold** gives one kingdom grounds for war.
- A score that rises far enough enables **formal alliance**, which carries mutual defence obligations.

**The player reads these thresholds as opportunities** — a relationship one modifier away from war is a target; a relationship one modifier away from alliance formation can be used to create a protective buffer.
