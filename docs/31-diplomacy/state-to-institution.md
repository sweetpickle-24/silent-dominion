# 31.3 State-to-Institution Relationships

Every kingdom has ongoing relationships with major institutions that operate within or near its territory:

- Banking houses.
- The Church or dominant religion.
- Major trade guilds.
- Scholarly academies.
- Military orders.

These relationships are **more operationally significant** than state-to-state relationships for the player because **institutions are where the player's owned entities live.**

## Relationship attributes

| **Relationship attribute** | **What it represents** | **Player exploitation** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Dependency score** | How much the kingdom relies on this institution for credit, legitimacy, or services | Higher dependency = more leverage. Increase dependency by making the kingdom rely on your institution more; decrease by introducing competitors. |
| **Trust score** | How much the kingdom trusts the institution's motives and reliability | Poison trust through scandal, failed deliveries, or rumoured mismanagement. Rebuild trust through demonstrated reliability. |
| **Debt balance** | How much the kingdom owes the institution in gold or silver | A kingdom in debt to your bank will not easily cross you. Engineering debt through timely loan offers during crises is a primary leverage-building tool. |
| **Political alignment** | Whether the institution's current leadership is seen as friendly, neutral, or hostile to the ruler | Install friendly leadership in your institutions through the coordinator layer. Use hostile alignment as a reason for the kingdom to turn to your competing institution. |
