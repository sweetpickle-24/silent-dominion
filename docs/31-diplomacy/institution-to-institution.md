# 31.5 Institution-to-Institution Relationships

Non-state actors have relationships with each other that are **largely invisible to anyone without deep network access.**

- Two competing banks have a rivalry score.
- A church and a banking house have a cooperation history.
- A guild and an academy share interests in specific ports.

These relationships create constraints and opportunities that neither state-level diplomacy nor individual host cultivation fully captures.

## Examples

- A bank that is in **conflict with the Church** cannot easily extend loans to a pious ruler without creating a political problem — the player can engineer this conflict to constrain a rival's financial operations.
- Two **guilds that cooperate** can coordinate market positions that individual manipulation cannot achieve — the player who owns both guilds can use this cooperation as an economic weapon.
- An **academic institution in good standing with a religious hierarchy** has legitimising reach that a secular academy cannot match — useful for propagating ideas that need both scholarly and ecclesiastical authority.
- **Rival secret societies** operate through their own institution networks — identifying which institutions a rival controls allows targeting their diplomatic leverage at the institutional level.
