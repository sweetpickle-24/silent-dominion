# 31.2 Casus Belli and War Mechanics

Kingdoms do not declare war arbitrarily. They need a **casus belli** — a justification credible enough for their subjects and allies to accept. The CB system determines whether a war is seen as legitimate aggression or naked invasion, which affects how other kingdoms respond and how the aggressor's own population accepts the costs.

## Key mechanics

- **Manufactured CB** — the player engineers an incident: a border raid falsely attributed to the target, a forged document showing territorial claim, a diplomatic insult that demands satisfaction. **The CB is artificial but the war is real.**
- **Suppressed CB** — a legitimate grievance exists but the player prevents it from being formalised — removing the advisor who would recommend action, ensuring the ruler's attention is diverted, bribing the council that would vote for war.
- **Alliance drag** — a war between two kingdoms pulls in all alliance partners. The player who triggers a war without checking the full alliance web may start a continent-wide conflict unintentionally. **Checking the alliance web is a standard pre-operation intelligence task.**
- **Marriage politics** — marriage negotiations between dynasties are a primary diplomatic instrument. The Weavers society specialises in this. The player can promote or block specific matches through court-level hosts, understanding that a marriage creates alliance obligations, succession claims, and cultural ties that last for generations.
