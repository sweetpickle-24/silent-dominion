# 9.1 Battle Resolution

Battles between forces are resolved by the simulation **without player input.** The player never commands troops or manages tactics. What they can do is have influenced the inputs beforehand — and the battle resolution reflects those inputs completely.

Every conflict evaluates a set of weighted factors to produce a probabilistic outcome that plays out over days of game time, with narrative events firing during the resolution.

## Battle factors

| **Factor** | **What it reflects** | **How the player can influence it** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Army size and quality** | Raw military strength; equipment, training, veteran experience | Elevate or degrade a general's reputation to affect recruitment; manipulate supply funding through economic threads |
| **General's trait scores** | Tactical ability, personal bravery, decisiveness under pressure | Direct host cultivation of the general; plant self-doubt or overconfidence to degrade performance |
| **Morale** | Pay status, recent win/loss record, disease, weather, political legitimacy of the cause | Seed rumours that undermine the cause; disrupt pay through economic manipulation; amplify grievances |
| **Supply lines** | Whether the army can sustain operations over days and weeks | Collapse a supply route through merchant network manipulation or indirect sabotage |
| **Terrain and season** | Geographic advantage; winter campaigns, river crossings, mountain passes | Cannot be directly manipulated — but the player can influence *when and where* an army chooses to march |
| **Paranoia and command dysfunction** | Whether the commander trusts their officers and makes coherent decisions | Amplify paranoia in a host to trigger fatal hesitation or premature aggression at a critical moment |

## Probabilistic outcome

Battle outcomes are **never certain.** A superior force can lose to a smaller one if morale collapses, a general hesitates, or supply fails at the wrong moment.

The player's influence on these factors is indirect and probabilistic — they improve or degrade odds, they do not guarantee results.
