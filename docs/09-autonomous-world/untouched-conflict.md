# 9.3 Autonomous Conflict Between Untouched Kingdoms

Kingdoms the player has **never interacted with** still war, form alliances, collapse, and produce dynasties. The simulation evaluates their relationships using the same tension and cycle mechanics that apply to player-influenced regions.

Two kingdoms that share a contested border, a history of dynastic rivalry, and competing trade interests will eventually go to war whether the player exists or not.

## Why this matters

This creates a world that is genuinely alive in the player's peripheral vision. The player may look up from a decade of careful work in the East to find that:

- The Western kingdoms have reshuffled completely.
- An empire they planned to use as a base has been absorbed.
- A minor city-state they ignored has grown into a regional power.
- A religion they never touched has become the dominant cultural force in half the map.

## Rules

- Untouched regions simulate at **lower fidelity** but still produce outcomes — wars, alliances, cultural shifts, economic cycles.
- When the player first extends intelligence into a region, the simulation's history of that region is **revealed retrospectively.**
- Player plans built around assumptions about untouched regions can be invalidated by what the simulation did without them.
- **This is a feature, not a failure** — the world's indifference to the player is what makes survival meaningful.
