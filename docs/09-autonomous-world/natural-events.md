# 9.2 Natural Events and Autonomous Change

The simulation runs a continuous background engine that generates natural events **independent of any political actor.** These events are not random — they are probabilistic outcomes of underlying simulation state.

- A drought is more likely after several dry years.
- A plague is more likely in a city with overcrowding and poor sanitation scores.
- A succession crisis is more likely when a ruler is old, has multiple ambitious heirs, and no established legal mechanism for succession.

## Event catalogue

| **Event type** | **Simulation inputs that drive it** | **Effect on the world** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Drought / famine** | Climate cycle state, agricultural investment, population density | Unrest surge, migration, weakened military recruitment, economic contraction |
| **Plague** | Trade route density, city sanitation score, population movement | Mass mortality, institutional collapse, power vacuums, accelerated succession |
| **Natural death of ruler** | Age, health score, lifestyle traits, stress index from court instability | Succession crisis, factional struggle, possible civil war if no clear heir |
| **Spontaneous war** | Border tension score, dynastic rivalry index, trade competition, recent insults | Fires when cumulative tension crosses a threshold; no single actor decides it |
| **Religious schism** | Accumulated doctrinal grievance, institutional corruption score, literate dissenter population | Splits a faith's political bloc; creates new ideological terrain |
| **Economic collapse** | Debt levels, trade route health, harvest failure, military spending overextension | Currency debasement, merchant class collapse, popular unrest, military unpaid |
| **Cultural flowering** | Accumulated wealth, political stability, literacy rate, philosophical tradition | Accelerates idea propagation, produces a generation of high-intellect procedural characters |
