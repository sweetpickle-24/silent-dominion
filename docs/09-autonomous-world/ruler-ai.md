# 9.4 Ruler AI — How Autonomous Actors Decide

Rulers, generals, advisors, and other powerful actors need behavioural logic for the moments the player is not involved — which is **most of the time, for most actors.**

The model is **goal-driven behaviour from trait-weighted preferences**, modulated by the influence of advisors who have the actor's ear at any given moment.

## Standing goals

Every powerful actor has a set of standing goals derived from their position.

- **A king's standing goals** always include: maintain power, secure the succession, protect the realm, and pursue whatever personal ambitions their traits weight highly.
- **A general's standing goals** always include: win the campaign they are committed to, build their reputation, manage their soldiers' loyalty.
- **A bishop's standing goals** always include: protect the institution, advance their faction within it, manage the spiritual welfare of their flock as they understand it.

## Decision inputs

| **Decision input** | **What it does** | **How it surfaces in play** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Standing goals** | Permanent priorities derived from the actor's position | Predictable baseline behaviour. A king will always defend his realm; a merchant will always pursue profit. |
| **Trait weights** | How the actor weights different responses to events based on personality | A high-paranoia king interprets ambiguous events as threats; a high-ambition king interprets them as opportunities. Same event, different decisions. |
| **Advisor influence** | Whichever advisors currently have the actor's ear push toward their own preferred responses | The militarist general advisor pushes for aggressive responses; the cautious chancellor pushes for diplomacy. The actor's decision is a weighted combination. |
| **Recent history** | Past decisions and their outcomes shape current willingness to act | An actor whose last war went badly is more cautious. An actor who recently triumphed is more confident. |
| **External pressures** | Famine, plague, succession risk, and other autonomous events shape what feels urgent | An actor in a region with widespread unrest cannot easily pursue ambitious foreign policy — internal stability dominates. |
