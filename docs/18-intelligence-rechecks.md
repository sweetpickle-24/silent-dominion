# 18. Intelligence Rechecks — Source Integrity

The player receives a report from a spy. Something about it does not add up. The numbers are too round. The timing is suspiciously convenient. The information is exactly what an enemy would want them to believe.

What they do next — and how they do it — is one of the game's **most tactically rich decision spaces.**

## 18.1 What triggers suspicion

Suspicion should not be arbitrary — it emerges from **contradictions in the information landscape.** The player notices that something does not fit, and that gap is the signal.

### Common triggers

- A spy's report contradicts a public news event about the same location or time period.
- Two sources covering the same ground report different facts without an obvious reason for the discrepancy.
- An action taken on a spy's intelligence failed in a way that implies the intelligence was structurally wrong, not just unlucky.
- A spy's reporting pattern has changed — suddenly more frequent, or suddenly vaguer, or suddenly more focused on specific topics.
- A report contains information that only someone wanting to manipulate the player would know to include.
- A rival society action in the same region suggests they knew something the player's spy should have reported but did not.

## 18.2 Investigation methods

Each investigation method trades speed against thoroughness and risk of detection. Critically, some methods can **alert a compromised spy** that they are being watched — which may cause them to clean up evidence or feed the same false information to the verification source.

| **Method** | **Speed** | **Detection risk** | **How it works** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Direct re-investigation** | Slow — weeks | Low if source is truly independent | New operative sent with no connection to original source. Verifies the specific intelligence independently. Thorough but expensive. |
| **Cross-reference check** | Fast — days | None | Suspicious data checked against all other available sources for contradictions. Only works if other sources cover the same ground. |
| **Source audit** | Medium — weeks | High if spy is compromised | The spy themselves is investigated rather than their report. Contacts, movements, recent behaviour examined for signs of compromise. Most revealing but riskiest. |
| **Consistency stress-test** | Variable | Medium | Act on the suspicious intelligence in a small, low-stakes way and observe whether it produces the expected result. Elegant but dangerous if the intelligence is badly wrong. |
| **Controlled leak test** | Slow — months | None to the spy | Feed the suspected source a unique piece of false information that only they receive. If it appears in enemy actions, the source is confirmed compromised. |

## 18.3 Resolution and confidence updates

An investigation does **not** return a binary verdict. It returns a **confidence update** — a probability shift in the source's reliability rating.

> 'Cross-reference found two contradictions. Source confidence reduced from 70% to 35%.'

The player then decides how to act on this new confidence level. Multiple investigations can be run sequentially, each narrowing the uncertainty further.

## 18.4 Response options

| **Response** | **What it involves** | **Risk and cost** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Quiet cut** | Stop feeding the source operational information. Let the relationship atrophy naturally. | Safest. Loses the source's value but creates no new risk. Correct choice if confidence is low but the source is not actively dangerous. |
| **Run as double agent** | Feed the source controlled false information. Monitor what the enemy does with it. | Aggressive and high-value if it works. Requires careful management through the coordinator. Constant risk of discovery. |
| **Confront through coordinator** | The coordinator meets the source and addresses the discrepancy directly. | Moderate risk. Useful if the source is innocent and scared. Dangerous if they are turned — they now know they are suspected and will report it. |
| **Remove the source** | The source is neutralised to prevent further damage. | Highest exposure. Justified only if the source is actively dangerous and the player has no time for subtler options. Always a last resort. |
| **Continue and monitor** | Keep the source active while surveillance is maintained. | Ongoing cost and risk. Appropriate while investigation is still running or while the double-agent play is being set up. |

## 18.5 The double agent as a weapon

A confirmed compromised spy is **not just a problem — it is an asset.** The player now has a direct channel into the enemy network that turned them. They control what the spy reports upward.

Used carefully, this is one of the most powerful tools available:

- Feed false intelligence to a rival society.
- Set up a sting that exposes their Coordinator.
- Identify other members of their network who contact the spy for information.

### Rules

- Running a double requires the coordinator to manage the deception on an ongoing basis — it consumes significant organisation bandwidth.
- The enemy may **test the double** by feeding them information that should produce a specific player reaction — if the player acts on the false intelligence, the double is burned.
- A successful double operation can last years and produce intelligence far more valuable than the original spy ever did legitimately.
- When the double is eventually burned, the enemy knows they were deceived and will purge every source that had contact with the double — **collateral damage must be anticipated.**
