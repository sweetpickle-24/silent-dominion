# 33.3 The Player's Lever Model

**The player never administers. They manipulate inputs.**

Every observable condition has a set of inputs the player can affect, and every action the player takes is aimed at one or more of these inputs.

The simplification principle is: **the player needs to understand what conditions they can create, not how the internal simulation produces those conditions.**

## Worked example — engineering a treasury crisis

| **Element** | **Detail** |
| --- | --- |
| **Target condition** | Kingdom treasury strained or broke |
| **Input levers** | Reduce trade income (disrupt trade routes through economic operations); increase military expenditure (seed a border conflict that forces mobilisation); reduce tax collection efficiency (amplify factional resistance to taxation through political hosts); accelerate debt service (engineer a diplomatic crisis that forces the kingdom to draw on reserves); trigger a crop failure (indirectly, through climate intelligence and timing an operation to coincide with a natural vulnerability period) |
| **Observable result** | Treasury condition degrades over months. Financial intelligence confirms the trend. The player is now in a position to offer a loan, extract concessions, or allow the crisis to produce the political instability they wanted. |
