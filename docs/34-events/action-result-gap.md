# 34.5 The Action-Result Gap

When the player takes an action, **they do not know the result.** They wait.

The result arrives through the three channels at different speeds in different forms. The player assembles the picture from fragments and contradictions.

**This gap is not a design flaw — it is the game's core epistemic experience.** Operating under genuine uncertainty about the outcomes of your own actions is what it means to be a shadow power.

## Operation-by-operation

| **Operation type** | **When the player learns the result** | **Through which channel** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Clean successful bribe** | Days to weeks post-operation | Operative report only. No public signal. The player knows their spy says it worked, but confirmation requires watching the target's subsequent behaviour. |
| **Failed bribe — silent rejection** | Days to weeks | Operative report of rejection. No neutral signal unless the target talks. Player loses the resource cost and knows the approach failed. |
| **Failed bribe — loud rejection** | Days (operative), weeks (neutral), possibly never (official) | Operative reports failure. Neutral channels carry a rumour that someone tried to buy the target. Official announcement possible if target chooses to publicise. |
| **Manufactured war** | Weeks to months depending on distance from conflict | Operative network first for immediate military movements. Official announcements of war declarations. Neutral channels carry the war's spread and impact. |
| **Compromised operation** | Often never directly — discovered through contradictions | Operative reports success. Neutral channels show unchanged or contradictory conditions. Eventually the misfire pattern reveals the compromise to a careful observer. |
| **Long-horizon ideological play** | Decades after the initial action | No immediate signal. Neutral channels eventually carry evidence of idea spread. The player recognises their own seeding in a movement they observe years later. |
