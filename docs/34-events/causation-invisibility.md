# 34.6 Causation Invisibility

The player cannot tell — and usually neither can any other actor — **whether a given event was caused by player action, rival society action, or autonomous simulation.**

- A famine might be natural, the Pyre's work, or the player's own grain supply disruption from three years ago finally reaching full effect.
- A king's sudden military campaign might be the result of a rival society stoking ambition, the player's own incitement operation from last year, or the natural consequence of the kingdom's improving treasury condition.

## Works in all directions

This causation invisibility works in all directions:

- The player cannot always tell what caused an event they observe.
- Other actors cannot tell what caused an event they observe.
- Critically, the player cannot tell from outside whether something they did was the cause of an outcome or whether the world just moved that way on its own.

## Design intent

This uncertainty is **not a frustration** — it is the texture of operating in history.

- Sometimes you cannot know if your action mattered.
- Sometimes you learn years later that it mattered enormously.
- Sometimes a plan you thought failed was actually the seed of something you observe a century on and suddenly recognise.
