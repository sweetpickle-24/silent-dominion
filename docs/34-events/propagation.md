# 34.2 Propagation Models

Every event propagates according to one of three models. Many events use a **combination.** The propagation model determines who learns what, when, and in what form.

## Geographic radial

The event spreads outward from its source location at a speed determined by the era's communication infrastructure.

> A battle in northern Gaul reaches Rome in days on Roman roads. It reaches Persia in weeks via merchant networks. It reaches China in months as a distorted rumour.

The content degrades with distance — distant recipients may hear the battle happened but not who won, or hear a distorted version of the outcome.

## Route-following

The event propagates along specific trade and road networks rather than radiating uniformly.

> A trade collapse propagates only to cities on the affected route and to merchants who use it. A landlocked kingdom with no connection to the relevant route may never hear, even if they are geographically closer than an informed coastal city.

The player's network coverage of specific routes determines whether they hear these events.

## Institutional

The event propagates through a **specific social or organisational network** regardless of geography.

> A papal decree reaches every bishop in the church within days no matter where they are. A guild ruling reaches every guild branch within its network. A secret society internal event reaches only members.

The player's membership or influence within these networks determines their access to institutional propagation channels.
