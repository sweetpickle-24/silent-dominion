# 4.1 Historical Divergence Model

Silent Dominion uses a **three-layer model** for handling history. The world does not follow a fixed script, but it does have momentum — and the player's actions bend, stretch, or eventually break that momentum.

## The three layers

### Layer 1 — Historical gravity

Events are attractors. Rome tends to rise. Religions tend to fill power voids. Without significant interference, history moves toward its documented form — though shifted in time and detail.

### Layer 2 — The cycle engine

Beneath named events are universal patterns. Republics rot into empires. Empires fragment along linguistic lines. Reformers become the thing they replaced. These patterns replay regardless of which civilisation is running them. See [Cycle Engine](./cycle-engine.md).

### Layer 3 — Full divergence

Cross a threshold of sustained interference and history **branches completely.** The named events stop being attractors. The simulation runs on pure cycle logic. You become the only being in the world who remembers what was supposed to happen.

## Design intent

- Early game feels historical. Players recognise the world.
- Mid game diverges where the player has acted heavily.
- Late game is genuinely alternate history — the world the player has made, not the world they found.
- The 100-generation arc ([§6.5](../06-progression-and-time/hundred-generations.md)) is the time frame in which full divergence can plausibly occur.
