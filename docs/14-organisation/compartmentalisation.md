# 14.2 Compartmentalisation as Protection

Every layer knows only what it needs to know and can expose only what it knows.

- An **Operative** caught and interrogated can give up their Coordinator at most.
- A **Coordinator** under pressure can expose their Lieutenant's existence but has no name, no face, no location to provide.
- A **Lieutenant**, even under the worst circumstances, knows only that the player exists — not who they are or where they are.

**The player's physical safety is structurally guaranteed as long as the architecture holds.**

## Cascade events

| **Event** | **Consequence** |
| --- | --- |
| **If an Operative is captured** | Their Coordinator is burned, the local cell is dissolved, the Coordinator rebuilds with new Operatives over months |
| **If a Coordinator is exposed** | The Lieutenant loses regional coverage temporarily and must rebuild from scratch in that area |
| **If a Lieutenant is compromised** | **Catastrophic.** The player must make direct contact to manage the crisis — which means exposure — and must find and cultivate a replacement from scratch |
| **If the player breaks compartmentalisation and contacts someone below Lieutenant level directly** | Every layer between them and that person becomes a potential exposure chain |
