# 25.1 Religion Attributes

| **Attribute** | **What it represents** | **How it affects gameplay** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Doctrinal rigidity** | How fixed and codified the religion's core beliefs are | High rigidity resists ideological manipulation but is brittle — a single doctrinal contradiction can trigger schism. Low rigidity bends but does not break. |
| **Institutional strength** | How organised and hierarchically developed the religion's governance is | Strong institutions are harder to infiltrate but more powerful once infiltrated. Weak institutions are porous but offer less leverage. |
| **Popular depth** | How deeply held the faith is at the lay level, separate from institutional authority | High popular depth means the faith survives institutional collapse. Low popular depth means the institution's fall takes the faith with it. |
| **Geographic concentration** | Where the faith is dominant, contested, or minority | Determines where the religion's power is usable and where schism or conversion is possible |
| **Reform potential** | Accumulated internal grievance against the institution or doctrine | High reform potential means schism is imminent without intervention. The player can accelerate, redirect, or prevent the schism. |
| **Ecumenical openness** | Willingness to engage with other faiths and philosophical traditions | High openness enables ideological cross-contamination. Low openness resists but becomes brittle when it cannot absorb challenges. |
