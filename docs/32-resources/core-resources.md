# 32.1 Core Resources — Fully Simulated

Core resources are tracked at **province level as production figures** and at **kingdom level as net balances.** Every kingdom monitors these and acts on deficits and surpluses. They are the primary levers for economic manipulation.

| **Resource** | **Primary source** | **Primary consumption** | **Strategic value** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Grain** | Fertile plains, river deltas, irrigated land | Population subsistence, army supply | Most critical resource. Shortage causes unrest, migration, and military collapse faster than any other deficit. |
| **Salt** | Coastal evaporation, mine deposits | Food preservation, livestock, trade | Historically a state monopoly. Control of salt supply gives leverage over every kingdom that cannot produce it internally. |
| **Iron** | Mountain and hill ore deposits | Military equipment, tools, construction hardware | Army quality depends directly on iron access. A kingdom cut off from iron cannot maintain its military equipment. |
| **Timber** | Forest provinces | Construction, naval, fuel, siege equipment | Naval power is entirely timber-dependent. A kingdom without timber cannot build a fleet regardless of its gold reserves. |
| **Silver** | Specific mountain deposits | Everyday currency, wages, small trade, taxation medium | The blood of a kingdom's daily economy. Silver shortage means soldiers go unpaid and markets freeze before the treasury notices. |
| **Gold** | Specific river and mountain deposits | Large transactions, treasury reserves, diplomacy, luxury | International reserve currency. Used for war funding, alliance payments, and debt settlement. Separate from silver in mechanics. |
| **Horses** | Plains and steppe provinces | Cavalry, transport, agricultural draught | Military composition determinant. A kingdom without horse provinces cannot field effective cavalry regardless of wealth. |
| **Cloth and textiles** | Sheep-grazing regions, flax cultivation, cotton (later eras) | Population clothing, army uniforms, trade commodity | One of the highest-volume trade goods across all eras. Control of textile production drives significant financial network capacity. |
