# 32.2 Secondary Resources — Era and Region Specific

Secondary resources matter significantly in specific eras and regions. They are tracked and traded but do not require the same simulation depth as core resources. Their primary game role is:

- Trade route value.
- Luxury economy.
- Era-specific strategic leverage.

| **Resource** | **Region and era** | **Trade significance** | **Player leverage** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Copper and tin** | Ancient era — bronze production regions | Essential for bronze-age military | Control bronze production to dominate ancient military supply |
| **Olive oil** | Mediterranean provinces | High-value food and fuel commodity | Mediterranean trade routes depend heavily on oil; disrupting olive groves devastates regional economies |
| **Wine** | Temperate Mediterranean regions | Luxury to everyday commodity by medieval era | High social value; court access and cultural influence through wine trade |
| **Spices** | Indian subcontinent, Southeast Asia | Highest-value trade commodity in medieval and early modern eras | Control of spice routes drove the age of exploration; whoever holds them holds a massive financial lever |
| **Silk** | China and Central Asia, later Mediterranean production | Enormous luxury value in western markets | Silk road control is a centuries-long strategic prize; disruption affects kingdoms dependent on eastern trade revenue |
| **Stone and marble** | Quarry provinces | Construction and prestige buildings | Controlling stone supply controls who can build fortifications and monumental architecture |
| **Livestock and meat** | Pastoral provinces | Food diversity, leather, bone goods | Secondary food supply; critical in regions where grain is scarce |
| **Fish** | Coastal and river provinces | Significant food supplement, especially for lean seasons | Coastal city food security; disrupting fishing access stresses urban populations |
| **Dyes** | Specific plant and shellfish sources | Textile value multiplier; purple historically reserved for royalty | High value, geographically concentrated; control gives cultural leverage |
| **Paper** | China early, Middle East and Europe later | Transforms information infrastructure when it replaces parchment | Paper spread accelerates idea propagation and increases literacy rate in affected regions |
| **Gunpowder components** | Saltpetre, charcoal, sulphur — late medieval onward | Military revolution enabler | Control saltpetre supply to constrain who can develop gunpowder military capacity |
| **Coal** | Specific geological deposits — early modern onward | Industrial and fuel revolution precondition | Coal control determines who industrialises first; enormous late-game strategic value |
