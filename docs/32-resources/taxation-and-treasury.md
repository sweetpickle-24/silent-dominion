# 32.5 Kingdom Taxation and Treasury — Simplified Model

Kingdoms run their own fiscal systems internally. **The player never manages this directly** — they observe conditions and exploit vulnerabilities. The simulation produces observable treasury states rather than precise numbers the player tracks.

## Treasury conditions

| **Treasury condition** | **What produces it** | **Player opportunity** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Flush** | Strong trade income, efficient tax collection, low military spending, no active debt | Little economic leverage. Target through other vectors. May be a good time to offer a loan with favourable terms to create future dependency. |
| **Stable** | Balanced income and expenditure, moderate debt, normal military costs | Baseline state. Monitor for shifts. Small disruptions will not destabilise. Large disruptions or crises may. |
| **Strained** | War costs, trade disruption, crop failure, high debt service, tax resistance | Active leverage window. A loan offer lands extremely well. Political concessions can be extracted. Rulers are more susceptible to promises of financial relief. |
| **Indebted** | Significant obligations to banks, church, or foreign creditors; interest consuming large share of income | Creditor leverage. If the player holds the debt, they can extract political compliance. If a rival holds it, the player can try to buy the debt or engineer the kingdom's default. |
| **Broke** | Cannot service debt, cannot pay army, markets seizing, emergency measures failing | Maximum crisis opportunity. Military loyalty collapses. Rulers accept deals they would normally refuse. Political instability is imminent. |

## Tax policy

Tax levels are a **ruler's policy decision** driven by their traits, advisors, and current fiscal needs.

- A high-ambition ruler running a war will push toward punishing taxation.
- A stability-focused ruler will keep taxes moderate.

The player can push tax levels in either direction by cultivating the right advisors and amplifying the right justifications.

- **High taxation** builds unrest.
- **Low taxation** builds loyalty but strains the treasury.

> The player who engineers punishing taxation in a kingdom they want to destabilise is **investing in future unrest rather than present damage.**
